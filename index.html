01

TripCraftClosed alpha

A personal travel app for every trip you take.

Why
About half of travellers now plan with AI, but fewer than 1 in 10 trust what it says. So every place in TripCraft is checked by a person before it reaches you.
Result
23,741 hand-checked places in 37 destinations, up from 11,846. Field-tested on a day trip with my kids.
Read the case

A separate, personalised companion app for each trip: an AI day-by-day itinerary grounded in hand-checked places, a Today view, a concierge that knows your itinerary, reservations, expenses, a journal with photo montage, destination guides, trending spots with honest verdicts, a taste profile, and offline access. Built for first-time international visitors and travelling families overwhelmed by fragmented planning tools.

Problem
40–56% of travellers use GenAI to plan a trip, but only 7–8% trust the output. The tools are fragmented, and families on a first international trip pay for every gap.
Key decision
Ground the product in places a person verified instead of scraping the web. “Every place in this app was checked by us before it reached you.” Run the AI layer against an eval board, not opinions.
Result
The verified catalogue doubled (figures below). An eval caught a token budget silently dropping trip-type rules on 36 of 64 prompts; after the fix, the targeted test cases went from 3 to 7 passing. The first full field day, a Mt Baker day trip with my kids, produced 20 observations. About 3,650 commits since May 2026, in closed alpha and active development.

  • 44 → 422curated reservation records, 37 destinations
  • 3 → 7targeted test cases passing after the fix

02

Kitchen JamOn TestFlight

Recipes that adapt to your pantry, your goals and your cravings.

Why
Most recipes fail on one missing ingredient. Kitchen Jam asks at most three questions, then rewrites the dish for what you have, and explains every change.
Result
No Kitchen Jam server exists: the AI runs on your iPhone or with your own key. Build 29 is on TestFlight, close to App Store submission.
Read the case
Problem
Home cooks drop a recipe over one missing ingredient, want a cuisine’s feel without its pantry, or need a dish to fit their macros.
Key decision
No server, ever: “there is no ‘us’ to send it to.” The model runs on device (Apple Foundation Models on iOS 27) or with a key you bring. A hard cap of three questions before the recipe card, and a “why it works” note for every change.
Result
A test harness grades six real requests against faults found in recipe cards testers actually cooked from. A WCAG audit moved the raspberry accent from 4.44:1 to 4.77:1 contrast. Build 29 is on TestFlight, near App Store submission.

Next.js 16 static export · TypeScript · Tailwind v4 · Dexie · Capacitor iOS · Apple on-device Foundation Models

03

Pocket PetHousehold beta, coming soon

A family pet that lives across your iPhone, not inside one app.

Why
Many apps have a pet. None let a whole family share one animal that knows each person, from the Lock Screen to the Apple Watch.
Result
My first decision was to cut my own spec. A review found four wrong iOS assumptions, so v0.1 is a small Phase 0 for one household.
Read the case
Problem
A pet in the Dynamic Island, an AI pet, a shared pet: all three exist. What does not is a persistent simulation with real family relationships, one that lives across the app, Live Activities, widgets, notifications and Apple Watch, and moves between phones.
Key decision
The pet is a simulation, not an LLM avatar. A review of my own spec concluded “build a small Phase 0; do not build the spec as written,” and found four wrong iOS platform assumptions, such as Live Activities lasting at most 8 hours and widgets animating for at most 2 seconds. No death, no guilt mechanics.
Result
v0.1.0, build 6, heading to a TestFlight household beta. Targets, not results yet: day-7 retention above 15%, day-30 retention above 8%, 30% of installs joining a household within 7 days, and owners describing the pet by behavior, not features.

04

Guitar IsolatorPrototype

Pull the guitar out of any song, with a draft tab to learn it.

Why
Learning by ear is hard when the guitar is buried in the mix.
Result
Tested against 360 real recordings. Notes on the right string went from 60% to 75% when learned rules replaced my hand-tuned ones.
Read the case
Problem
Guitarists learning a song by ear need the guitar part on its own: pulled out of the mix, split when there are several, with key, tempo and chords alongside.
Key decision
Score every tab against GuitarSet (360 recordings, six players) before trusting any opinion. Replace hand-tuned string costs with learned ones. Honest scope on the page itself: tabs are an editable draft.
Result
Right-string accuracy on players the model had not seen rose from 0.60 to 0.75. Bend detection went from almost never right (0.06 precision, 0.03 recall) to 0.36 and 0.36. CREPE was tried for bends, gave no gain, and was reverted. The Guitar Pro importer is checked on 31 real files. Runs as an invite-only server for friends on a Mac Mini.

Python · Demucs / PyTorch · basic-pitch · librosa · scikit-learn · FastAPI · Web Audio (vanilla JS) · Claude

06

The SearchIn development

A Ramayana-inspired action adventure: Rama and Lakshmana search the forest for Sita.

Why
One chapter of the Ramayana as a 20–30 minute game for players 8 and up on a high-end phone: fast combat with hero switching, a light-and-stone puzzle, and a moral beat where the player sees another character’s perspective.
Result
All five slice milestones are merged and a full run works from the opening to the end card, at 60 FPS on iPhone 17 Pro, with about 600 automated tests and 240 commits in its first week.
Read the case
Problem
Tell one chapter of the Ramayana as a game without changing the story. After Sita’s abduction, Rama and Lakshmana search the Dandaka forest, defeat and free the cursed Kabandha, meet Shabari, and end when Hanuman meets them at Pampa Lake. The events are fixed; the player still needs real choices.
Key decision
Moral beats show perspective, not outcomes: at Shabari’s berries the player sees her side, and the story ends the same way. The forest beat doubles as the guided, skippable tutorial. The greybox level was chosen by a bake-off between two model lanes, built in overnight agent sprints where one model implements and another reviews.
Result
Milestones S1 to S5 merged; a full run from the opening to the end card; 60 FPS on iPhone 17 Pro at the first milestone check; 413 EditMode and 182 PlayMode tests; 240 commits since 3 October 2026. Rama’s 3D model v5 is in the game and environment art is in progress. Next: tuning after phone playtests, and a cultural review of all text.

Unity 6 · URP · C# · iOS · AI-assisted concept art · Agent lanes: one model implements, another reviews

How I work

I started my career as a product engineer, spent years in strategy consulting, and now lead payments platform product. With AI agents, the core of the PM job (precise specs, clear acceptance criteria, reviewing work against intent) became the scarce input for building software. Four decisions show how that works in practice.

TripCraft

The bug no test could see

Every test was green, yet packing lists failed for any long, cold or family trip. The model’s answer was longer than a token cap, so its JSON was cut off mid-string. A hundred and fifty test files could not see it, because the code was correct. The nightly eval caught it on its first scheduled run, and the same measure went from 0% to 99% inside two hours.

Tests catch exceptions. Evals catch embarrassments.

TripCraft

Generated is not the same as true

Much of the travel catalogue was researched with AI help. When a separate pass tried to disprove each claim, only 59% survived. That number changed the pipeline: every place now has to survive a refutation pass and a human check before a traveller sees it. It is why TripCraft can say that every place was checked before it reached you.

Build the fact-check into the pipeline, not the postmortem.

Pocket Pet

The spec I did not build

My first Pocket Pet spec was, in the words of its review, a five-year studio roadmap. Four of its iOS assumptions were wrong: a Live Activity lasts at most 8 hours, and a widget animates for at most 2 seconds. The verdict was to build a small Phase 0 with a kill criterion and a budget. I cut to a dog and a cat, no server, and my own household as the first test group. The family pet, the best idea in the spec, stayed.

The cheapest time to be wrong is before the first line of code.

Guitar Isolator

The popular tool I removed

Bends were the weakest part of the draft tabs, so I tried CREPE, a well-known pitch tracker. Against checked GuitarSet labels it made vibrato detection worse, left bends poor, and added about 10 seconds per minute of audio. I removed it. A small classifier trained on GuitarSet replaced the hand-written bend rules instead and raised bend precision from 6% to 36%.

A popular tool is not a result. Keep what the numbers support.
